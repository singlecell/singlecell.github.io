Research

Three directions — AI-driven intelligent analysis, in situ mass spectrometry, and quality and safety evaluation of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM).

I develop new mass-spectrometry technologies and instrumentation for analysing complex traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) systems. By integrating in situ mass spectrometry, miniature mass spectrometry and artificial intelligence, my group has built a systematic in situ MS platform for TCM. The work runs along three mutually reinforcing directions: AI-driven intelligent analysis turns spectra and images into decisions, in situ mass spectrometry changes how and where we measure, and quality and safety evaluation of TCM applies both to medicinal materials and preparations.

AI-Driven Intelligent Analysis

Turning spectra and images into decisions

Fusing machine learning with in situ and miniature mass-spectrometry data to build intelligent interpretation and discrimination methods for TCM, and applying deep learning to routine analytical images such as TLC plates, so that raw data turn quickly and reproducibly into identifications and quantitative results. The tools are open source.

Intelligent spectral interpretation

Machine-learning models built on in situ and miniature MS data recognise characteristic ions and discriminate between samples.

On-site authentication

Miniature MS plus machine learning authenticates agarwood (genuine, Kynam, counterfeit) and citrus herbs on site.

Image-based quantitation

The same approach extends to TLC images — TLC-RAPID locates spots with YOLOv5 instance segmentation and back-calculates amounts from same-plate standards.

Open-source tools

AgarwoodMiniMS and TLC-RAPID ship as Windows applications with source code and demo data.

In Situ Mass Spectrometry

Bringing the measurement to the sample

Developing a family of in situ techniques — TLC coupled with ambient MS, integrated chromatography–bioautography–MS screening, and MS imaging for spatial analysis — that identify TCM constituents directly, without sample preparation; and new ionization and on-site qualitative and quantitative methods tailored to portable miniature mass spectrometers, taking analysis from the lab into the field.

TLC–ambient MS

Graphite-assisted LA-DART-MS, DART and LESA ionize constituents straight from TLC plates, adding sensitivity while keeping the separation.

Integrated bioactivity screening

TLC–bioautography–ambient MS yields chemical and biological profiles from a single plate.

Mass spectrometry imaging

Spatially resolved maps of constituents and metabolites in tissue sections link chemistry to biology.

Miniature MS on site

New ionization such as paper capillary spray, matched to portable miniature MS, enables on-site testing of efficacy and toxic constituents.

Quality and Safety Evaluation of TCM

From medicinal materials and preparations to standards

Combining in situ and miniature MS with high-resolution and ion-mobility LC–MS to characterise constituents and to evaluate the quality and safety of medicinal materials and preparations — applied to ginseng, notoginseng, agarwood, aged citrus peel, Senecio and others. As project leader, I led the development of the international standard ISO 19842:2025 for Chinese yam (Shanyao).

Comprehensive characterization

High-resolution and ion-mobility LC–MS (LC–MS / IM-MS) speed up the identification and annotation of complex multi-component systems.

Quality evaluation

Quality evaluation and authentication of ginseng, notoginseng, agarwood, aged citrus peel and their preparations, including how constituents transform during processing.

Safety screening

Rapid identification and quantitation of pyrrolizidine alkaloids in Senecio and other herbs, and spatial lipidomics of the liver damage they cause.

International standards

Project leader of the international standard ISO 19842:2025 on Chinese yam (Shanyao); expert of ISO/TC 249.
